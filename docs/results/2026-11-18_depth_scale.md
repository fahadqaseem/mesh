## 1. Depth scale correction

**Method (no GT anywhere in the fit;** `src/photo2mesh/points/depth_scale.py`, `scripts/depth_scale.py`**).** VGGT's depth maps are multiplied by k (one per scene, or k_i per view) with VGGT's cameras and the known focal fixed. For 20,000 fused pixels per view (the pixels the TSDF uses, default filter), each pixel of view i is back-projected with k_i and projected into every other view j; where it lands on a fused pixel of j, the relative depth residual r = (k_j d_j - z_j) / z_j is read with bilinear interpolation. Cost = mean of min(|r|, eps) over all ordered view pairs, so pixels that see different surfaces (occlusion) cost eps whatever k is. Global k: grid 0.90-1.10 (step 0.005, then 0.0005). Per view: coordinate descent from the global k (3 sweeps, ±0.03). Then the default TSDF (`scripts/tsdf_points.py --depth-scale`), 1M points, camera alignment as protocol v1. About 23 s per shape on the CPU. Unit test: a synthetic sphere with depth scaled by 0.97 is recovered to 0.1% (`tests/test_depth_scale.py`).

**Choice:** global k with eps 0.005, the only fitted setting that does not lose Chamfer on pencilpot (6.19 to 6.18;
F1@0.01 0.37 to 0.49, outliers > 0.01 58.5% to 46.0%). Per-view scales were worse at every eps, and larger eps fit
larger k. The fixed-k probe shows that **the fit overshoots**: the signed offset is zero near k = 1.0125, the fit gives
1.0165. It also shows a trade-off no k removes: outliers > 0.01 fall up to k ~ 1.0125, but outliers > 0.02 and
recall get worse as soon as k > 1.005, so the far outliers are not a depth-scale error.

**Interpretation.**
- **The fit confirms the bias without GT:** on 10 of 12 set B shapes the views agree best with longer depth (k 1.004 to
  1.037; 1.000 on thingi_290506 and 0.995 on 94668), and the shapes with the largest k are the ones ICP shrank most (thingi_103356 1.037,
  593381 1.035).
- **But it overcorrects and only helps where the offset is large.** The mean signed offset goes from +0.0065 to -0.0022
  (sign flipped on 10 of 12), as on pencilpot. Means: F1@0.01 +0.03 and outliers > 0.01 -4.2 points, but Chamfer +0.22
  (better on 6 of 12) and outliers > 0.02 +0.8. Large gains on the two worst shapes (thingi_103356: Chamfer 8.40 to 7.57,
  F1@0.01 0.14 to 0.53, outliers > 0.02 55% to 32%; thingi_593381: 7.81 to 7.60), losses on thin shapes: on
  thingi_77949 (Chamfer 5.53 to 8.71) the scaled depth from opposite views no longer meets on thin parts, the TSDF
  carves them, and 17% of the extracted surface is dropped as small pieces (6% before).
- **It is far from the ICP bound** (Chamfer 4.68, outliers > 0.02 7.0%). ICP with scale shrinks the whole cloud about
  its centre; a depth scale only moves points along each view's rays. The two are only loosely related (correlation
  0.45), so most of what ICP removes is not a depth-scale error. The remaining suspect is the focal length: VGGT's own
  focal estimate is 4-11% shorter than the true one on every shape (`cameras.npz` intrinsic_pred), and we combine its
  depth with the true focal. A GT-free joint fit of depth scale and focal (same agreement cost) is the next test.
- **Decision:** not adopted as the default (mixed per shape, worse mean Chamfer). Per-view k is not better than global.

**Interpretation.**
- **Low views reach the gaps.** Swapping views for low ones fills 36% (low A) and 27% (low B) of the GT surface the
  current 8 views miss, more than 16 views from the usual two rings (22%), and raises recall all @0.02 on 8 (A) and 10 (B)
  of 12 shapes. This confirms the 17 Nov finding that the gaps are unseen surface (undersides), not filtered pixels.
  Unlike the Gaussian gap fill (46% filled, +4 points of outliers > 0.02), these are observed points: outliers > 0.02
  go down slightly (-1.2 and -0.5 points).
- **But one set of 8 views cannot cover both top and bottom.** The effect is large and goes both ways: thingi_77949
  (Chamfer 5.53 to 4.22, recall 0.48 to 0.93) and thingi_73155 (3.74 to 2.55) gain a lot from low A, while
  thingi_1146183 (8.54 to 10.68), 105924 and 103356 lose, because low A drops the 50 deg ring and their tops go unseen.
  Mean Chamfer is slightly worse for both sets (+0.25, +0.06). Low B (2 / 4 / 2) is the more balanced choice.
- The signed offset grows with low views (+0.0065 to +0.010 / +0.009): the depth bias of section 1 does not depend on
  the elevation.
