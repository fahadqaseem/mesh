# Gap filling: TSDF points plus Gaussian-kernel points only where the TSDF surface is missing

Fahad Qaseem Khawar, 14 Nov 2026. Tables regenerated by `scripts/fill_report.py`; the interpretation at the end is written by hand. Points scored against the GT with protocol v1 (as in `docs/results/2026-11-12_points.md`); MeshFlow is the secondary row. Set A = 7 original shapes, set B = 12 new shapes (clean test set); pencilpot is the dev shape (both rules were chosen on it) and is in no mean.

Queue: 139 of 139 jobs ok.

**Rule** (`scripts/fill_points.py`, `src/photo2mesh/points/fill.py`, no GT used): keep every TSDF point (200k) and add the Gaussian-kernel samples (`_gsk`, 200k, opacity-weighted) whose distance to the nearest TSDF point lies in a band, as a fraction of the TSDF cloud's largest extent: far enough to be a gap, close enough not to be a floater. Primary band 3-10% (best Chamfer on pencilpot), secondary 2-5% (best recall on pencilpot). A neighbour-count filter against isolated samples made pencilpot worse and is off.

## Interpretation

Written 14 Nov after the queue finished (139 of 139 jobs ok, 19 MeshFlow runs).

- **Gap filling gives a better point cloud than TSDF alone.** Primary rule (kernel samples 3-10% from the TSDF
  cloud, about 20k added to 200k), set B: Chamfer 5.61 to 5.34 (better on 8 of 12), F1@0.02 0.708 to 0.732 (11 of
  12), coverage 76% to 82%, all-points recall @0.02 0.677 to 0.794 (12 of 12), NC unchanged (0.809 to 0.808). Set A
  shows the same direction (Chamfer 5.58 to 5.42, better on 6 of 7; recall better on 7 of 7). The largest gains are on
  the shapes where TSDF is worst (thingi_103356 recall @0.02 0.47 to 0.82, thingi_593381 Chamfer 7.78 to 6.95).
- **The cost is a small, consistent loss at the tightest tolerances.** F1@0.01 drops by 0.004 (set B, better on
  only 1 of 12) and F1@0.005 by 0.008: the added points are less precise than the TSDF surface, as the diagnostic
  predicted (only 15-40% of kernel points in TSDF gaps lie within 2% of the GT). They fill holes coarsely; they do not
  add fine detail.
- **The two bands are close.** 2-5% adds more points (55k on set B) for more recall (0.817) and a slightly lower NC;
  3-10% has the better Chamfer and F1@0.02, as on pencilpot. The primary stays the 3-10% band.
- **Why the diagnostic looked worse than the result:** most kernel points far from the TSDF are off the GT, but the
  protocol's Chamfer and F1@0.02 reward the GT surface that becomes covered more than they penalise points a few
  percent off it. Hausdorff does not get worse (21.7 to 21.4) because the 10% upper limit drops the far floaters.
- **MeshFlow (secondary) gets worse with the filled cloud**, like every input containing kernel points: set B Chamfer 6.24 to
  6.70, NC 0.819 to 0.773, 1,284 vertices instead of 933. MeshFlow's random 4,096 now include ~10% kernel points, and
  their scatter costs more than the coverage gains at its 32^3 resolution. So the best cloud and the best MeshFlow input are
  no longer the same cloud.

**Decision (my call, safest):** report the gap-filled cloud (3-10%) as the best points output; the pipeline default
and MeshFlow's input stay the plain TSDF cloud. Whether the points default switches (it needs the 3DGS step, ~10 s
per object and not bit-reproducible) is for Fahad and Prof. Zhong.
